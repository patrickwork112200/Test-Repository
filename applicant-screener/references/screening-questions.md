# Screening Question Bank

Use these when converting unknowns and flags into the report's **Probe
questions** section. Pick and adapt — a good probe list is 6–10 questions
targeted at *this* candidate's specific unknowns, not a generic script. Order
them: knockouts first (cheapest to resolve, ends the call early if failed),
then comp/logistics, then motivation, then skill depth, then flags —
behavioral last, they take the longest.

If an answer discloses protected information (health, family status,
religion, and the rest of the SKILL.md list), acknowledge briefly, steer back
to the job-relevant part of the question, exclude the disclosure from the
evaluation, and do not record it in notes or the report. What *is* usable is
the job-relevant residue: "available from March" is usable; why they are
available from March is not.

## Knockout & logistics (first 5 minutes)

- "Are you legally authorized to work in [location] without sponsorship?"
- "The role is [onsite/hybrid X days/remote] in [location] — does that work
  for you, and what's your commute/relocation situation?"
- "This role runs on [shift/schedule] — have you worked that schedule before,
  and is it sustainable for you?"
- "What's your notice period, and is any of it negotiable or buyout-able?
  When could you realistically start?"

## Compensation (early, matter-of-fact)

- "So we don't waste your time: the budget for this role is [range/up to X,
  basic]. Does that work for you?" — when the user allows disclosing budget;
  disclosure is the fastest honest filter.
- "What's your current compensation — basic and total package — and what are
  you expecting for your next move?" — where local law permits asking current
  comp; several US states/localities ban the question, in which case ask only
  expectations.
- "Is that expectation based on basic or total package? What does your
  current package include — allowances, bonus, HMO/benefits?"
- "How firm is that number — is there a range you'd consider for the right
  role?"

## Motivation & acceptance risk

The screen isn't only "can they do it" — it's "will they accept, join, and
stay long enough to matter." An endorsed candidate who declines the offer
costs the user a client-facing miss; probe this dimension whenever motivation
data didn't arrive with the materials:

- "What's prompting the move from [current company]?" — listen for pull
  ("this role offers X") vs. pure push ("anywhere but here"); push-only
  motivation raises counteroffer and early-attrition risk.
- "What specifically interests you about this role/client?" — a candidate
  who can't answer is applying everywhere; note it as acceptance risk, not
  as a score.
- "Are you interviewing elsewhere? Any offers in hand or expected soon?" —
  pipeline risk shapes how fast the user must move.
- "If your current employer counters with more money, what happens?" — asked
  matter-of-factly; hesitation is information.
- "What would make you turn this offer down?" — surfaces the real
  dealbreaker (shift, commute, title) while it can still be addressed.

Findings land in the report's Risks section as acceptance/pipeline risk with
a recommended response (expedite the loop, pre-close on comp, address the
stated dealbreaker) — they modify urgency and conditions, not the score.

## Skill-depth probes

The pattern: pick the criterion scored on thin evidence, ask for the last
concrete instance, then drill one level down. Specifics collapse quickly when
the claim is hollow.

- "Your CV lists [skill]. Tell me about the last time you used it in
  production — what did you build, and what went wrong?"
- "Walk me through [named project] — what was *your* part specifically, versus
  the team's?"
- "You worked with [system] at [company] — what scale? Users, data volume,
  transactions?"
- "If I asked your last tech lead what your strongest and weakest area is,
  what would they say?"
- For seniority claims: "How many people did you lead? Hire/fire authority?
  What's a decision you made that your manager only found out about later?"

## Tenure, gap & flag probes

Phrase neutrally — you're collecting the explanation, not prosecuting:

- "Walk me through the move from [A] to [B] — what prompted it?"
- "I see a gap between [X] and [Y] — what were you doing during that time?"
- "The [role] at [company] was about 8 months — was that a contract, or what
  happened?"
- "Your CV says [claim]; help me square that with [inconsistency]."
- For the vague-consulting gap: "Which clients did you work with, and what
  did you deliver? Anyone we could speak to?"

## Behavioral (STAR) — for must-have soft competencies

Ask for a *specific past instance* and walk it through Situation → Task →
Action → Result; hypothetical answers ("I would…") are a prompt to redirect:
"Tell me about a time you actually did."

- Stakeholder management: "Tell me about a time a client/stakeholder pushed
  for something you believed was wrong. What did you do?"
- Pressure/deadlines: "Tell me about the tightest deadline you've hit — and
  one you missed. What happened after?"
- Conflict: "Tell me about a disagreement with a teammate or lead that had
  real consequences. How did it resolve?"
- Ownership: "Tell me about a mistake you made in production. Who found it,
  and what did you do?"
- Learning velocity (for stretch hires): "Tell me about the last skill you
  had to learn fast because a project demanded it."

Listen for: specific names/numbers/timelines, the candidate's individual
actions (I vs. we), and results with aftermath. Coached or fabricated answers
are generic, hero-narrated, and consequence-free.

## Authenticity checks (remote hiring)

- Camera on for at least part of every round; consistent identity across
  rounds (same person, same background story).
- One unscripted moment per call: a follow-up that no prepared answer covers
  ("you mentioned X — what would you have done if it hadn't worked?").
- Long answer latency plus perfectly-structured prose can mean answers are
  being generated or fed; slow the cadence, interrupt politely mid-answer,
  ask them to go off-script (whiteboard, "explain like I'm new").
